# Common Ground: real-estate model review
Reviewed October 20, 2026.

## Assessment
Useful as an illustrative feasibility and conversation tool. It is not a transaction-ready underwriting, origination, securities placement or legal-document platform. No independent licensed professional has certified this review.

## Corrections made
- Property taxes, insurance, HOA and vacancy are explicit editable inputs. Default values remain hypothetical, not local quotes.
- Monthly P&I is distinguished from broader housing costs. Mortgage insurance and household debt are not included in an underwriting determination.
- Contractual rent credits are separated from modeled lender-accepted purchase funds. New records default to zero rent-credit acceptance; the upfront payment acceptance remains a visible assumption.
- Exit appraisal is independent of proposed purchase price. Modeled LTV is applied to the lower of appraisal and price.
- Junior-lender recovery now includes senior interest during the workout delay. Taxes, protective advances, default interest and enforcement costs beyond the entered allowance remain unmodeled.
- Seller cash now, deferred principal and note payments are distinguished. Principal returned is not profit.
- Public-facing language identifies the concept as independent from Zegacy and the records and partners as fictional.
- Browser-local storage is disclosed: sharing the demo does not share the user's deal records.

## Real transaction conditions
Confirm actual mortgage type and transfer permissions; seller release; junior-financing acceptability; title and existing liens; appraisal and market rent; buyer capacity and eligible funds; insurance and occupancy; broker compensation; seller-financing and originator requirements; servicing; option and credit enforceability; extension, refund and default rights. Record references do not authenticate lender approvals.

## Important model limits
The junior-loan analysis and investor property-acquisition analysis are separate exposures. Both use deterministic scenarios, not default probabilities. Property-acquisition IRR assumes a purchase exit and must not be presented as a guaranteed yield. The rental fallback excludes settlement of refundable upfront funds or rent credits. The buyer financing stress conservatively treats unaccepted credits as unavailable funds rather than automatically assuming an offsetting price reduction; actual purchase-price adjustments require lender and contract reconciliation. Tax effects, legal remedies and investor suitability are outside this prototype.

## Sharing
The downloadable common-ground-demo.html contains the complete prototype and fictional starting records. It can be sent as a file and opened in a browser. It is not a hosted URL or a shared multi-user database. The private Sites source upload was rejected by automatic approval review, so no deployed URL has been verified.
